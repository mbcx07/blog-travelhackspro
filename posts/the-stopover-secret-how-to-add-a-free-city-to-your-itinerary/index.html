The Stopover Secret: How to Add a Free City to Your Itinerary

2026-05-29 · Flights · Tags: flights, travelhackspro

Imagine booking a flight from New York to Paris and getting a free stopover in Reykjavik, Iceland—or flying from Los Angeles to Bangkok and spending a day in Tokyo without paying an extra cent. This isn't a fantasy; it's the power of the stopover. While most travelers view layovers as tedious waits in sterile airport terminals, savvy travelers know that a well-planned stopover can transform a single journey into a two-for-one adventure. In this guide, we'll unlock the stopover secret, showing you exactly how to add a free city to your itinerary, save money, and make your next long-haul flight an unforgettable multi-destination experience.

What Is a Stopover and Why It's a Game-Changer

A stopover is a deliberate pause in your journey that lasts more than 24 hours (though some airlines define it differently). Unlike a layover, which is typically a short connection under four hours, a stopover gives you enough time to leave the airport, explore a city, and even stay overnight—all without increasing your airfare. Many airlines offer stopovers as a perk to encourage tourism in their hub cities, and they often cost nothing extra. This means you can effectively visit two destinations for the price of one flight.

The benefits go beyond just saving money. Stopovers break up long-haul flights, reducing jet lag and fatigue. They allow you to experience a new culture, taste local cuisine, and see iconic landmarks—all while your luggage continues to your final destination. For example, Icelandair has long offered free stopovers in Reykjavik on transatlantic flights, letting travelers soak in the Blue Lagoon or chase the Northern Lights before continuing to Europe. Similarly, TAP Air Portugal offers a free stopover in Lisbon or Porto on many routes, and Singapore Airlines has a stopover program that includes hotel discounts and attraction passes. These are not just marketing gimmicks; they are genuine opportunities to enrich your travels.

How to Find Stopover-Friendly Airlines and Routes

Not all airlines offer stopovers, and even those that do may have specific rules. The key is to know which carriers are stopover-friendly and how to search for them. Here are some of the most generous airlines:

  • Icelandair: Free stopover in Reykjavik on flights between North America and Europe.
  • TAP Air Portugal: Free stopover in Lisbon or Porto on flights from North America, Africa, and Brazil to Europe.
  • Singapore Airlines: Stopover in Singapore with exclusive hotel rates and attraction passes.
  • Finnair: Stopover in Helsinki on flights between Asia and Europe, often with free transit tours.
  • Turkish Airlines: Stopover in Istanbul with free hotel nights for certain fare classes.
  • Qatar Airways: Stopover in Doha with discounted hotel packages.

To find these deals, start by searching on the airline's official website rather than third-party aggregators. Look for a "Stopover" or "Multi-City" option in the booking engine. Alternatively, use Google Flights and select "Multi-city" to manually add a stopover, but be aware that not all airlines will price it the same. A better approach is to visit the airline's stopover page directly—many have dedicated portals that let you add a stopover at no extra cost. For instance, Icelandair's website has a simple toggle to add a stopover, and TAP's "Stopover" section lets you choose your free night in Lisbon.

The Booking Process: Turning a Layover into a Stopover

Booking a stopover is easier than you think, but it requires a shift in how you search for flights. Instead of looking for the cheapest nonstop or one-connection itinerary, you need to intentionally build in a longer connection. Here's how to do it:

First, decide on your final destination and your desired stopover city. Then, use the airline's multi-city search tool to enter your origin, stopover city, and final destination as separate segments. For example, if you're flying from Chicago to Rome and want a stopover in Reykjavik, you'd search Chicago to Reykjavik, then Reykjavik to Rome. The total price should be similar to a direct Chicago-Rome flight, often even cheaper because the airline is routing you through its hub.

If the airline doesn't have a dedicated stopover program, you can still create one by booking a multi-city ticket with the same airline or its partners. The trick is to ensure the stopover is at least 24 hours long—otherwise, it's just a layover. When you book, pay attention to the fare rules. Some airlines allow free stopovers on international itineraries, while others may charge a small fee. Always compare the multi-city price with a round-trip price to confirm you're not overpaying.

Another pro tip: Use the airline's "stopover" filter on search engines like Skyscanner or Kayak. These platforms often highlight stopover deals, but they may not show all options. For the best results, combine your research with the airline's official stopover page, which often includes exclusive perks like hotel discounts, free tours, or even meal vouchers.

Maximizing Your Stopover: What to Do with 24 to 48 Hours

Once you've secured your stopover, the real fun begins. A 24-hour stopover is enough to see a city's highlights, but you need a plan. Start by checking if your airline offers a stopover package—many include hotel deals, airport transfers, and attraction passes. For example, Singapore Airlines' stopover program includes a free shuttle to the city, a discounted hotel, and a hop-on-hop-off bus ticket. Take advantage of these perks to save both time and money.

If you're on your own, prioritize what you want to see. For a short stopover, focus on one or two neighborhoods rather than trying to see everything. In Reykjavik, you can walk the colorful streets, visit the Hallgrímskirkja church, and soak in a geothermal pool—all within a few hours. In Lisbon, ride the historic Tram 28, explore the Alfama district, and enjoy a pastel de nata at a local café. In Istanbul, visit the Hagia Sophia and Grand Bazaar, then take a Bosphorus cruise.

Packing for a stopover requires a bit of strategy. Since your main luggage is checked through to your final destination, you'll need a small carry-on with essentials: a change of clothes, toiletries, and any medications. Wear comfortable shoes and layers, as you'll be walking and the weather may differ from your origin and destination. Also, consider booking a hotel near the airport if your stopover is short—many airlines have partnerships with nearby hotels that offer day-use rooms, so you can rest without venturing far.

Potential Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

While stopovers are fantastic, they come with a few caveats. The most critical is visa requirements. Even if you're just passing through, you may need a transit visa for certain countries. For example, a stopover in the United States requires a visa or ESTA, even if you're not leaving the airport. Always check the entry requirements for your stopover country well in advance. Some countries, like Singapore, offer visa-free transit for up to 96 hours, but others are stricter.

Another pitfall is luggage. If you book a stopover as a single ticket, your bags will typically be checked through to your final destination, but you won't have access to them during the stopover. That's fine if you've packed a carry-on, but if you need something from your checked bag, you'll have to retrieve it—which may require rechecking and could invalidate your stopover. To avoid this, always pack a small overnight bag in your carry-on.

Timing is also crucial. A stopover of less than 24 hours might not give you enough time to leave the airport, especially if you factor in immigration, customs, and travel to the city. On the other hand, a stopover longer than 72 hours might require a separate ticket, which could increase costs. Aim for 24 to 48 hours—enough to see the highlights without feeling rushed.

Finally, be aware of airline policies. Some airlines require you to book a specific fare class to qualify for a free stopover, and others may charge a fee if you change your stopover dates. Read the fine print before booking, and if you're unsure, call the airline's customer service to confirm.

Advanced Stopover Hacks: Combining with Loyalty Programs and More

For the truly savvy traveler, stopovers can be combined with loyalty programs to unlock even more value. Many airlines allow you to earn miles on all segments of your stopover itinerary, and some even offer bonus miles for stopover bookings. For example, TAP Air Portugal's stopover program often includes extra miles for members. Additionally, you can use your miles to upgrade your stopover hotel or book a longer stay.

Another advanced hack is to use stopovers to break up ultra-long-haul flights. If you're flying from New York to Sydney, a stopover in Honolulu or Fiji can make the journey more bearable and give you a mini-vacation in the middle. Some airlines, like Qantas, offer stopover packages in their hub cities, but you can also create your own by booking a multi-city ticket with a partner airline.

Finally, consider combining stopovers with open-jaw itineraries. For instance, you could fly into Lisbon, stopover for two days, then continue to Paris, and fly home from London—all on one ticket. This gives you three destinations for the price of a round-trip, and with careful planning, you can maximize your time and minimize your costs. The key is to be flexible with your dates and routes, and to always compare the total price against a standard round-trip.

Conclusion

The stopover is one of the most underutilized travel hacks in the industry. It allows you to see more of the world without spending more money, and it can turn a mundane flight into an exciting adventure. By choosing stopover-friendly airlines, booking strategically, and planning your time wisely, you can add a free city to your itinerary on almost any long-haul trip. So next time you're searching for flights, don't just look for the fastest connection—look for the one that gives you a bonus destination. Your future self will thank you for the extra stamp in your passport.
