The Stopover Strategy: How to Turn a Layover into a Second Destination

2026-06-30 · Hacks · Tags: The, hacks

Most travelers view a long layover as an inconvenience—a few boring hours stuck in an airport terminal, waiting for a connecting flight. But what if that same layover could become a free mini-vacation, letting you explore an entire city without paying an extra cent for airfare? That's the power of the stopover strategy. Airlines around the world offer stopovers—intentional breaks of 24 hours or more—at no additional cost, often on long-haul international routes. By understanding how stopovers work and how to book them, you can turn a simple trip from Point A to Point B into a two-for-one adventure. In this guide, I'll show you exactly how to leverage stopovers to see more of the world for the same price, avoid common pitfalls, and even earn extra loyalty points along the way.

Layover vs. Stopover: Know the Difference

Before diving into the strategy, it's crucial to understand the distinction between a layover and a stopover. A layover is a short connection, typically under 24 hours, where you remain in transit and your luggage is checked through to your final destination. A stopover, on the other hand, is a deliberate pause of 24 hours or more, often lasting a few days. Airlines treat these differently: layovers are usually free and automatic, while stopovers may require specific booking techniques or may be included as a perk on certain routes.

The key is that many airlines allow stopovers on round-trip or multi-city itineraries without charging extra airfare. For example, if you're flying from New York to Bangkok, you might be able to stop in Tokyo for two nights at no additional cost—you just need to know how to book it. This isn't a hack; it's a published rule, but most travelers never take advantage of it because they default to the fastest route.

The Best Airlines and Routes for Free Stopovers

Not all airlines offer free stopovers, but several major carriers have made it a cornerstone of their business model. Here are the standouts:

  • Icelandair: Stop in Reykjavik for up to 7 days at no extra cost on transatlantic flights between North America and Europe. This is one of the most famous stopover programs.
  • TAP Air Portugal: Stop in Lisbon or Porto for up to 5 days on flights between Europe, Africa, and the Americas. TAP even offers a "Portugal Stopover" package with hotel discounts.
  • Finnair: Stop in Helsinki for up to 5 days on flights between Asia and Europe or North America. Helsinki is a compact city perfect for a short visit.
  • Emirates: Stop in Dubai for up to 2 nights with their Dubai Stopover package, which includes hotel and meal vouchers at discounted rates.
  • Qatar Airways: Stop in Doha for up to 4 nights with their Qatar Stopover, offering hotel deals and transit visas.
  • Singapore Airlines: Stop in Singapore for up to 7 days on certain routes, with special hotel rates and a free city tour for transit passengers.

These are just a few examples. Other carriers like Air Canada (via Toronto or Vancouver), Turkish Airlines (via Istanbul), and Ethiopian Airlines (via Addis Ababa) also offer stopover perks. The trick is to search for itineraries that include a long connection and then check the airline's stopover policy.

How to Book a Stopover Without Paying Extra

Booking a stopover isn't as simple as choosing a flight with a long layover—you need to use the right tools and techniques. Here's a step-by-step approach that has saved me hundreds of dollars on multi-city trips:

Use multi-city search on booking engines. Instead of searching for a round-trip, use the multi-city option on Google Flights, Kayak, or Skyscanner. Enter your departure city, then your stopover city as a destination, and then your final destination. For example, search New York to Reykjavik to London as a multi-city, then compare the price to a direct New York to London flight. Often, the multi-city fare is identical or only slightly higher, especially if the stopover is on the same airline.

Check airline stopover programs directly. Many airlines have dedicated pages on their websites where you can add a stopover to an existing booking. For instance, Icelandair allows you to add a stopover to any transatlantic itinerary during the booking process—just select the "Stopover" option. TAP and Emirates have similar features. If you can't find it online, call the airline's reservation desk and ask for a stopover quote.

Leverage alliance partnerships. If you're flying with a Star Alliance, OneWorld, or SkyTeam carrier, you might be able to add a stopover on a partner airline at no extra cost. For example, a United flight to Asia can include a stopover in Seoul on Asiana Airlines, but you'll need to book through United's multi-city function or call their reservations.

Watch out for taxes and fees. While the airfare might be the same, stopovers can incur additional airport taxes or surcharges, especially if you're staying longer than 24 hours. Always compare the total price, including taxes, before booking. In many cases, the stopover is still cheaper than booking a separate flight to that city.

Making the Most of Your Stopover: Time and Budget Planning

Once you've secured your stopover, the real fun begins. But a 24-hour stopover can feel rushed if you don't plan ahead. Here are my tips for maximizing a short visit:

  • Choose a city with a compact center. Reykjavik, Helsinki, Lisbon, and Doha are all walkable or have efficient public transit, so you can see major sights in a few hours.
  • Book a hotel near the airport or city center. For overnight stopovers, look for hotels with free shuttle services or easy rail connections. Many airlines have stopover packages that include discounted hotel rates—take advantage of them.
  • Use airport luggage storage. If you're only stopping for a day and don't want to drag your bags around, most airports have luggage storage facilities. You can drop your bags and explore hands-free.
  • Plan a flexible itinerary. Don't over-schedule. Pick one or two must-see attractions and leave time for wandering. For example, in Reykjavik, you can visit the Hallgrímskirkja church and the Harpa concert hall, then relax in a geothermal pool.
  • Check visa requirements. Some countries require a transit visa even for short stays. For example, a stopover in the UK might require a visa if you're not from a visa-exempt country. Always verify before booking.

Hidden Costs and Pitfalls to Avoid

Stopovers can be a fantastic deal, but they come with potential pitfalls that can turn your mini-vacation into a nightmare. Here's what to watch out for:

Baggage handling. If you have checked luggage, it will usually be checked through to your final destination on a stopover, but not always. Some airlines require you to collect your bags at the stopover point, especially if the stopover exceeds 24 hours. Confirm this at check-in and pack a small carry-on with essentials in case you need to access your bags.

Immigration and customs. Even if you're not leaving the airport, you may need to go through immigration if your stopover is in a different country. This can be time-consuming, so factor in at least 2-3 hours for the process.

Missed connections. If your stopover is too short (e.g., 12 hours), you might not have enough time to leave the airport and return for your next flight. Always leave a buffer of at least 8 hours for a city visit, and 12+ hours for an overnight stay.

Additional costs. While the flight is free, you'll pay for meals, transportation, and possibly a hotel. However, these costs are often far less than booking a separate flight to that city. Use the airline's stopover packages to get discounts on hotels and tours.

Combining Stopovers with Loyalty Programs

If you're a frequent traveler, stopovers are a goldmine for earning extra miles and status. Here's how to stack the benefits:

Earn miles on the stopover segment. Even if you're not flying the stopover airline for the entire itinerary, you'll earn miles for each segment flown. For example, a New York–Reykjavik–London itinerary on Icelandair will earn miles for both legs, and you might also earn bonus miles for the stopover if you book through a partner airline.

Use stopovers to qualify for elite status. Adding a stopover increases your total miles flown, which can help you reach a higher tier faster. Many travelers use stopovers strategically to boost their year-end mileage total.

Leverage lounge access. If you have elite status or a premium credit card, you can enjoy airport lounges during your stopover. This is especially valuable if you have a long layover before your connecting flight. Some lounges even offer showers and sleeping pods, turning your stopover into a restful break.

Book with a co-branded credit card. Many airline credit cards offer free checked bags, priority boarding, and even a free stopover benefit. For example, the Icelandair Saga Club credit card gives you a free stopover on every round-trip ticket.

Conclusion

The stopover strategy is one of the most underutilized travel hacks in the industry. By intentionally building a long layover into your itinerary, you can explore an extra city for the price of your original flight—sometimes even cheaper than a direct route. The key is to know which airlines offer free stopovers, how to book them using multi-city search, and how to plan your time efficiently. Whether you're hopping across the Atlantic with Icelandair or transiting through Dubai on Emirates, a stopover can transform a mundane journey into a memorable adventure. So next time you book a long-haul flight, don't settle for the shortest connection. Embrace the layover, turn it into a stopover, and double your travel experiences without doubling your budget. Happy travels!
